Final Assessment Evidence Index

FICTIONAL / EDUCATIONAL ASSESSMENT

«Important: This evidence index is part of a fictional and educational GRC portfolio assessment based on DesertTech Solutions, a fictional UAE technology company.

The evidence described below represents the types of evidence that would be required to substantiate the assessment conclusions. It does not represent actual evidence obtained from a real organization.»

---

1. Purpose

The purpose of this evidence index is to identify the evidence that would be required to validate the risks, control weaknesses, compliance gaps, findings, and recommendations documented in the final GRC assessment.

The assessment conclusions should not be treated as proven solely because they are documented in the portfolio.

In a real assessment, each conclusion would need to be supported by appropriate, reliable, and traceable evidence.

---

2. Evidence Principles

Evidence should be:

- Relevant to the requirement or control being assessed
- Sufficient to support the conclusion
- Reliable and obtained from an appropriate source
- Current enough for the assessment period
- Traceable to the relevant system, process, control, or owner
- Protected from unauthorized modification
- Clearly dated where applicable
- Reviewed for completeness and authenticity

Where evidence is unavailable, the assessor should record the limitation rather than assuming that a control is operating.

---

3. Evidence Required for Risk Conclusions

R-001 — Inappropriate or Excessive Access

To substantiate the access-management risk, a real assessment would require evidence such as:

- Current user access listings
- Privileged-account listings
- Role/access matrices
- Access-review records
- Joiner/mover/leaver records
- Terminated-user access reports
- Access approval records
- Evidence of access removal
- Identity and access-management system reports
- Periodic access-review schedules

What the evidence would demonstrate

The evidence should establish whether:

1. Users have appropriate access.
2. Privileged access is controlled.
3. Access reviews actually occur.
4. Excessive access is identified.
5. Access is removed when no longer required.

---

4. R-002 — Overdue Security Actions

Evidence required:

- Security action register
- Vulnerability-management reports
- Internal audit findings
- Penetration-test findings where applicable
- Remediation tickets
- Risk-treatment plans
- Action owners
- Target dates
- Closure evidence
- Management escalation records

What the evidence would demonstrate

The evidence should establish which security actions are open, how long they have been outstanding, who owns them, and whether completed remediation has actually been validated.

5. R-003 — Vendor Risk

11. Evidence Required for Control Testing

For controls identified as operating effectively, a real assessment should obtain evidence appropriate to the control.

Examples include:

Control Area| Example Evidence
Access management| Access review sample and approval records
Privileged access| Privileged-account review
Vulnerability management| Scan results and remediation records
Security awareness| Training completion records
Incident management| Incident records and response exercises
Vendor risk| Vendor assessments
Backup| Backup logs and restore-test results
Business continuity| Recovery exercise results
Change management| Approved change records
Policy governance| Approved policy and review history
Logging| Security-monitoring records
Privacy| Data inventory and privacy assessments

Evidence should be tested against the actual control objective rather than collected simply because it exists.

---

12. Evidence Required for Compliance Conclusions

For any compliance conclusion, the assessor should obtain:

1. The exact applicable requirement.
2. Confirmation that the requirement applies to the organization.
3. The organization's corresponding control or process.
4. Evidence that the control exists.
5. Evidence that the control operates.
6. Evidence of exceptions or failures.
7. Evidence of remediation where gaps exist.

A compliance conclusion should therefore follow:

Requirement → Control → Evidence → Assessment → Finding → Remediation

---

13. Evidence Required for Internal Audit Findings

To substantiate an internal-audit finding, evidence should normally establish:

- The expected requirement
- The assessment criteria
- The actual condition observed
- The evidence supporting the condition
- The affected scope
- The potential impact
- The responsible owner
- The agreed corrective action
- The target completion date
- Closure/validation evidence

This prevents findings from being based only on assumptions or undocumented observations.

---

14. Evidence Required for Vendor Findings

For a vendor-related finding, the assessment should obtain evidence showing:

- Which vendor was assessed
- Why the vendor is considered important
- What systems/data the vendor can access
- Vendor risk classification
- Due-diligence results
- Contractual security requirements
- Identified weaknesses
- Risk owner
- Treatment decision
- Remediation status
- Reassessment requirements

15. Evidence Required for Privacy Conclusions

Privacy-related conclusions should be supported by evidence such as:

- Personal-data inventory
- Processing activities
- Data owners
- Purpose of processing
- Access information
- Retention requirements
- Data-sharing records
- Third-party processors
- Security safeguards
- Privacy notices/policies
- Data-subject request processes where applicable

- Deletion/disposal evidence where applicable

The exact evidence required should be determined according to the applicable privacy law, regulatory requirements, contractual obligations, and business context.

---

16. Evidence Required for Management Recommendations

Management recommendations should be supported by:

- Risk assessment results
- Control-gap findings
- Compliance requirements
- Evidence of current weaknesses
- Business impact analysis
- Existing remediation status
- Resource considerations
- Risk priority
- Assigned ownership
- Proposed timelines

This allows management to understand why an action is recommended rather than receiving an unsupported list of tasks.

---

17. Evidence Sufficiency Test

Before relying on evidence, the assessor should ask:

Relevance

Does the evidence actually relate to the control or conclusion?

Reliability

Can the source reasonably be trusted?

Completeness

Does the evidence cover the required population or period?

Currency

Is the evidence recent enough?

Traceability

Can the evidence be connected to the specific requirement or control?

Authenticity

Can the origin and integrity of the evidence be reasonably established?

If these questions cannot be answered, the conclusion may need to be qualified.

---

18. Evidence Gaps

If required evidence is unavailable, the correct GRC response is not to invent evidence.

Instead:

1. Record the evidence gap.
2. Identify what evidence is missing.
3. Determine whether the missing evidence affects the assessment conclusion.
4. Communicate the limitation.
5. Assign an owner if evidence needs to be produced.
6. Establish a target date.
7. Reassess when appropriate evidence becomes available.

---

19. Portfolio Evidence Policy

All evidence used in this portfolio should be clearly classified as one of the following:

- Fictional
- Simulated
- Educational
- Sanitized, where applicable

No confidential company information, real customer information, personal information, credentials, secrets, or proprietary organizational records should be included.

---

20. Final Evidence Traceability Model

The intended evidence chain is:

Business Requirement
        ↓
Risk / Compliance Requirement
        ↓
Control
        ↓
Evidence Requirement
        ↓
Evidence Collected
        ↓
Evidence Tested
        ↓
Assessment Result
        ↓
Finding
        ↓
Risk Treatment
        ↓
Remediation Evidence
        ↓
Retesting
        ↓
Management Reporting

This approach demonstrates that GRC conclusions should be evidence-based and traceable, rather than based on assumptions.

---

21. Final Statement

The conclusions in the DesertTech Solutions case study are educational assessment conclusions based on the fictional scenario provided.

In a real engagement, the conclusions would require the evidence described in this index to be obtained, validated, tested, and appropriately documented before making formal claims about control effectiveness, compliance, audit readiness, or risk status.

No evidence should be fabricated to make an assessment appear stronger than it is.
