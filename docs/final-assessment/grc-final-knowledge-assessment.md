GRC Final Knowledge Assessment

Purpose

This assessment tests practical understanding of Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) concepts across major security and compliance areas.

The answers are written in original wording for portfolio and learning purposes. They are not copied definitions.

Scenario: DesertTech Solutions — fictional organization used for educational portfolio exercises.

---

1. ISO 27001

Q1. What is the main purpose of ISO/IEC 27001?

Answer:
ISO 27001 provides a structured way for an organization to establish, operate, maintain, and continually improve an Information Security Management System (ISMS). Its main value is not simply having security controls, but managing information-security risks systematically and demonstrating that security is governed and continually improved.

Q2. What is an ISMS?

Answer:
An ISMS is the management structure an organization uses to control information-security risks. It brings together policies, responsibilities, risk management, controls, monitoring, audits, corrective actions, and continual improvement rather than treating security as a collection of isolated technical activities.

Q3. Why are Clauses 4–10 important in ISO 27001?

Answer:
Clauses 4–10 describe the management-system requirements that make the ISMS work. They cover organizational context, leadership, planning, support, operations, performance evaluation, and improvement. Together, they create the management cycle around information security.

Q4. What is a Statement of Applicability (SoA)?

Answer:
The SoA is the organization's documented explanation of which applicable ISO 27001 Annex A controls are included, excluded, implemented, or planned. The important part is the justification behind those decisions, because controls should be selected based on the organization's risks and circumstances.

2. NIST CSF

Q5. What is the NIST Cybersecurity Framework used for?

Answer:
NIST CSF gives organizations a practical structure for understanding and improving cybersecurity risk management. I would use it to compare the current security position with a desired target position and identify areas that need improvement.

Q6. What is the difference between a current profile and a target profile?

Answer:
A current profile represents where the organization's cybersecurity practices are today. A target profile describes where the organization wants those practices to be. Comparing the two helps identify gaps and prioritize improvement work.

Q7. Why is NIST CSF useful for GRC professionals?

Answer:
It helps connect technical cybersecurity activities with business risk. A GRC professional can use it to organize findings, communicate security maturity to management, and create practical improvement priorities without focusing only on individual security technologies.

---

3. Risk Management

Q8. What is information-security risk?

Answer:
Information-security risk is the possibility that a threat could exploit a weakness and cause an unwanted business impact. I would consider both how likely the event is and how serious its consequences would be.

Q9. What is the difference between a threat and a vulnerability?

Answer:
A threat is something capable of causing harm, such as an attacker, malware, or accidental action. A vulnerability is a weakness that could be exploited or otherwise contribute to that harm. For example, phishing can be a threat while inadequate user awareness can be a vulnerability.

Q10. What is inherent risk?

Answer:
Inherent risk is the level of risk before considering the effect of existing controls. It helps an organization understand the exposure created by a business activity in its relatively untreated state.

Q11. What is residual risk?

Answer:
Residual risk is what remains after existing controls and planned risk treatments are taken into account. It is important because eliminating every risk is usually unrealistic, so management needs to understand what exposure remains.

Q12. Why should risk owners be assigned?

Answer:
A risk owner gives accountability to someone who has enough authority and business understanding to decide how the risk should be handled. Without an owner, risks can remain documented but unmanaged.

4. Risk Treatment

Q13. What are the main ways to treat risk?

Answer:
An organization can generally avoid the risky activity, reduce the risk through controls, transfer or share some of the risk, or accept the remaining risk when it falls within an approved tolerance. The correct option depends on business impact, cost, regulatory requirements, and risk appetite.

Q14. What is a risk treatment plan?

Answer:
A risk treatment plan turns identified risks into actions. It should show what treatment is required, who owns it, what control or action will address it, when it should be completed, and how progress will be monitored.

Q15. Why shouldn't every risk automatically be treated?

Answer:
Risk treatment consumes resources, and some risks may already be within the organization's acceptable tolerance. The goal is informed risk management rather than trying to eliminate every possible risk regardless of cost or business value.

---

5. Compliance

Q16. What is compliance in a GRC context?

Answer:
Compliance means meeting applicable laws, regulations, contractual requirements, standards, policies, and other obligations. A GRC professional helps translate those requirements into controls, evidence, responsibilities, monitoring activities, and corrective actions.

Q17. What is a compliance gap?

Answer:
A compliance gap exists when the organization's current practice does not fully satisfy an applicable requirement. The gap should be documented clearly, supported with evidence, assigned to an owner, and tracked toward remediation.

Q18. Why is evidence important for compliance?

Answer:
Evidence provides something concrete that demonstrates whether a requirement or control is actually operating. Without evidence, an organization may claim that a control exists but have difficulty proving its implementation or effectiveness.

6. Internal Audit

Q19. What is the purpose of an internal audit?

Answer:
An internal audit provides an independent and structured review of whether processes and controls are working as intended and meeting applicable requirements. It can identify weaknesses before they become larger business or compliance problems.

Q20. What is an audit finding?

Answer:
An audit finding is a documented result from comparing expected requirements with what was actually observed. Depending on the situation, it may identify conformity, an opportunity for improvement, or a nonconformity that requires corrective action.

Q21. Why should auditors maintain objectivity?

Answer:
An auditor needs enough independence to evaluate evidence honestly rather than simply confirming that management's expectations are correct. Objectivity makes audit conclusions more credible and useful.

---

7. Control Testing

Q22. What is control testing?

Answer:
Control testing checks whether a control exists, is properly designed, and is operating as expected. Depending on the control, testing could involve reviewing documents, inspecting configurations, sampling records, interviewing responsible personnel, or observing a process.

Q23. What is the difference between design effectiveness and operating effectiveness?

Answer:
Design effectiveness asks whether the control is capable of addressing the relevant risk. Operating effectiveness asks whether that control actually operated consistently and appropriately during the period being reviewed.

Q24. Why is sampling used in control testing?

Answer:
Sampling allows an auditor or tester to examine a reasonable selection of records without manually reviewing every transaction. The sample should be selected using a defensible approach appropriate to the control and assessment objective.

---

8. Evidence

Q25. What makes good audit evidence?

Answer:
Good evidence should be relevant, reliable, understandable, traceable, and appropriate for the requirement being tested. It should allow another reviewer to understand what was checked and why it supports the conclusion.

Q26. What is an evidence index?

Answer:
An evidence index is a structured list connecting requirements or controls to their supporting evidence. It helps auditors and control owners quickly locate documentation and identify missing evidence.

Q27. Why should portfolio evidence be labelled as fictional?

Answer:
Because professional GRC work can involve confidential organizational information. Clearly identifying portfolio material as fictional or educational demonstrates ethical handling of information and prevents the work from being mistaken for evidence from a real organization.

9. Policies

Q28. What is the purpose of an information-security policy?

Answer:
An information-security policy establishes management's expectations and direction for protecting information. It provides a high-level foundation for responsibilities, security objectives, required behavior, and supporting procedures and controls.

Q29. What is the difference between a policy and a procedure?

Answer:
A policy explains what the organization requires and the direction management has established. A procedure explains how a particular activity should actually be performed. For example, a password policy can establish requirements while an account-management procedure explains the operational steps.

Q30. Why should policies have owners and review dates?

Answer:
An owner provides accountability for keeping the policy relevant. A review date ensures that changes in technology, regulations, business operations, or risks can be considered rather than allowing an outdated document to remain active indefinitely.

10. Privacy

Q31. How is privacy different from information security?

Answer:
Information security focuses broadly on protecting information from unauthorized access, alteration, loss, or disruption. Privacy focuses specifically on how personal information is collected, used, stored, shared, retained, and otherwise processed in accordance with applicable requirements and individual rights.

Q32. What is data minimization?

Answer:
Data minimization means avoiding collection or retention of personal information that is unnecessary for the legitimate purpose. From a GRC perspective, reducing unnecessary data can also reduce the organization's privacy and security exposure.

Q33. Why should a privacy assessment consider the data lifecycle?

Answer:
Privacy risks can occur at every stage, from collection through use, sharing, storage, retention, and deletion. Looking at the entire lifecycle makes it less likely that an organization will protect data during one stage while overlooking risks elsewhere.

11. UAE Compliance

Q34. Why is UAE-specific compliance knowledge valuable for a UAE GRC analyst?

Answer:
A UAE GRC professional needs to understand the regulatory and security environment relevant to organizations operating in the country. Knowledge of frameworks such as the UAE NCA ECC and applicable privacy requirements helps make compliance work more locally relevant.

Q35. What is the UAE NCA ECC?

Answer:
The NCA ECC is a cybersecurity control framework used in the Saudi/UAE-region context?

Correction: The UAE National Cybersecurity Authority's regulatory framework should be distinguished carefully from other countries' cybersecurity authorities. In a UAE portfolio, I should verify the exact authority, framework version, and organizational applicability before claiming that a specific control requirement applies.

Q36. Why should a GRC analyst verify regulatory applicability?

Answer:
Not every regulation or framework applies to every organization. Applicability can depend on factors such as sector, organization type, data handled, regulatory jurisdiction, contractual obligations, and government requirements. A GRC analyst should verify scope rather than assuming every requirement applies.

12. Vendor Risk

Q37. What is third-party risk management?

Answer:
Third-party risk management is the process of identifying and managing risks created by suppliers, vendors, contractors, cloud providers, and other external parties. The assessment should continue throughout the relationship rather than stopping after onboarding.

Q38. What should vendor due diligence examine?

Answer:
Depending on the vendor's risk level, due diligence can examine security controls, privacy practices, certifications, incident history, business continuity, subcontractors, access to data, contractual obligations, and other relevant risks.

Q39. Why should vendors be classified by risk?

Answer:
Not every vendor creates the same level of exposure. A vendor processing sensitive information or having privileged system access generally deserves more extensive due diligence and monitoring than a low-risk supplier with no access to organizational information.

13. BCP / Disaster Recovery

Q40. What is the difference between BCP and disaster recovery?

Answer:
Business continuity planning focuses on keeping critical business services operating or restoring them within acceptable limits during disruption. Disaster recovery is more focused on recovering technology, systems, applications, and data that support those services.

Q41. What are RTO and RPO?

Answer:
RTO is the maximum acceptable time for restoring a service or system after disruption. RPO describes how much data loss, measured in time, the organization can tolerate. For example, a one-hour RPO means the organization aims not to lose more than roughly one hour of data.

---

14. Incident Management

Q42. What is the purpose of an incident-management process?

Answer:
Incident management provides an organized method for detecting, reporting, analyzing, containing, responding to, recovering from, and learning from security incidents. The objective is to reduce damage and restore normal operations while preserving useful information for investigation.

Q43. Why is incident documentation important?

Answer:
Documentation creates a reliable record of what happened, what actions were taken, who was responsible, and what lessons were identified. It can support investigations, regulatory obligations, management reporting, and future improvements.

---

15. GRC Metrics

Q44. What makes a GRC metric useful?

Answer:
A useful metric should help someone make a decision. Instead of simply counting activities, I would prefer measures that show risk, control performance, compliance status, remediation progress, or trends that management can act upon.

Q45. Give examples of useful GRC metrics.

Answer:
Examples include overdue high-risk findings, percentage of controls tested on schedule, percentage of critical vendors assessed, average remediation time, policy review completion, audit finding closure rate, and the number of high-risk issues past their target dates.

---

16. Governance

Q46. What is governance in cybersecurity?

Answer:
Governance establishes who has authority, who is accountable, what objectives have been approved, how decisions are made, and how security performance is overseen. It connects cybersecurity activities with business priorities and management responsibility.

Q47. What is the difference between governance and management?

Answer:
Governance provides direction, accountability, oversight, and decision authority. Management is more focused on executing that direction through processes, resources, controls, and operational activities. In practice, strong GRC requires both.

17. Microsoft / Purview

Q48. How can Microsoft Purview support GRC and compliance?

Answer:
Microsoft Purview can help organizations manage areas such as data governance, information protection, compliance, records, and risk-related activities across Microsoft environments. From a GRC perspective, it can help translate compliance requirements into operational monitoring and data-governance activities.

Q49. What is the value of Microsoft Compliance Manager?

Answer:
Compliance Manager can help organizations assess compliance posture against selected regulatory or standards-based requirements, identify improvement actions, assign responsibility, and track progress. It can therefore provide a bridge between compliance requirements and practical remediation work.

---

18. Integrated GRC Thinking

Q50. If you discovered a high-risk control gap, what would you do?

Answer:
First, I would validate the finding and make sure the evidence supports it. Then I would document the risk, affected process or asset, business impact, existing controls, and risk owner. I would agree on an appropriate treatment and target date, track the remediation, collect evidence showing the corrective action was implemented, and retest where appropriate. Finally, I would update the risk and compliance records and report the status to the appropriate stakeholders.
