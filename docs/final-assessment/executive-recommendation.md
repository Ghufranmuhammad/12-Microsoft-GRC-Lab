Executive Recommendation

DesertTech Solutions — Management Security & GRC Readiness Recommendation

«FICTIONAL / EDUCATIONAL PORTFOLIO

DesertTech Solutions is a fictional UAE technology company. This recommendation is a simulated management presentation created for educational and portfolio purposes. It does not represent a real consulting engagement, audit, certification, regulatory assessment, or customer assessment.»

---

1. Executive Summary

DesertTech Solutions is preparing for a major customer security assessment. The current review indicates that the organization has several important GRC and security weaknesses that should be addressed before the assessment.

The most significant concerns are:

1. Incomplete user and privileged-access reviews
2. No formal vendor-risk management program
3. Overdue security remediation actions
4. Inconsistent security evidence and assessment readiness
5. Insufficiently mature privacy and business-continuity governance

Management should prioritize risks according to their potential business impact rather than attempting to fix everything simultaneously.

The immediate objective should be to reduce critical exposure, establish accountability, and produce reliable evidence demonstrating that controls are operating.

---

2. Top 5 Risks

Rank| Risk| Priority| Business Impact
1| Inappropriate or excessive system access| Critical| Unauthorized access, data exposure, fraud, customer trust impact
2| Unmanaged third-party/vendor risk| Critical| Supplier-related security incident, privacy exposure, service disruption
3| Overdue security actions| High| Known weaknesses may remain exploitable
4| Customer personal-data exposure| High| Privacy incident, regulatory/contractual consequences, reputational damage
5| Business-service disruption| High| Customer downtime, revenue impact, SLA/contractual consequences

---

Risk 1 — Inappropriate or Excessive Access

Priority: Critical

Incomplete access reviews create the possibility that employees, contractors, or privileged users have access they no longer require.

Business Justification

A compromised or unnecessary account could provide an attacker with access to sensitive systems or customer information. This risk is particularly important because DesertTech processes customer data and relies heavily on cloud infrastructure.

Recommended Management Decision

Direct the security and IT teams to complete a risk-based access review, beginning with privileged and high-impact systems.

---

Risk 2 — Unmanaged Vendor Risk

Priority: Critical

DesertTech currently has no formal vendor-risk management program.

Business Justification

A security weakness at a supplier can become a security problem for DesertTech even when DesertTech's internal controls are working correctly. Vendors may have access to systems, customer information, or critical services.

Recommended Management Decision

Approve the creation of a formal third-party risk-management process and prioritize critical suppliers.

---

Risk 3 — Overdue Security Actions

Priority: High

Several security actions have exceeded their expected completion dates.

Business Justification

Known weaknesses represent understood exposure. Allowing high-risk actions to remain overdue increases the likelihood that an existing weakness will contribute to a security incident.

Recommended Management Decision

Require risk-based prioritization and management escalation for overdue high and critical security actions.

---

Risk 4 — Customer Personal-Data Exposure

Priority: High

DesertTech processes customer personal data and requires stronger visibility into how that information is collected, accessed, stored, shared, retained, and protected.

Business Justification

A privacy incident could affect customers, contractual relationships, regulatory obligations, and the organization's reputation.

Recommended Management Decision

Establish a clear data inventory and review privacy controls and third-party processing arrangements.

---

Risk 5 — Business-Service Disruption

Priority: High

DesertTech depends heavily on cloud infrastructure and external providers.

Business Justification

A major technology or supplier disruption could prevent customers from receiving services and potentially create contractual and financial consequences.

Recommended Management Decision

Validate business continuity and disaster recovery arrangements for critical services and conduct appropriate recovery testing.

---

3. Top 5 Control Weaknesses

Rank| Control Weakness| Priority| Primary Owner
1| Incomplete access reviews| Critical| IAM / IT Security
2| No formal vendor-risk lifecycle| Critical| GRC / Procurement
3| Weak remediation tracking and escalation| High| Security Management
4| Inconsistent evidence management| High| GRC
5| Immature privacy and continuity governance| High| Privacy / BCP Owners

---

Weakness 1 — Access Review Process

Current Weakness:
Access reviews are incomplete and do not provide sufficient assurance that all access remains appropriate.

Expected Improvement:
A documented, repeatable review process covering normal, privileged, transferred, and terminated-user access.

---

Weakness 2 — Vendor-Risk Lifecycle

Current Weakness:
There is no formal process covering vendor identification, classification, due diligence, approval, monitoring, and reassessment.

Expected Improvement:
A risk-based third-party lifecycle with additional scrutiny for vendors handling sensitive information or supporting critical services.

---

Weakness 3 — Remediation Governance

Current Weakness:
Several security actions are overdue.

Expected Improvement:
Centralized tracking with risk ratings, owners, deadlines, escalation rules, and management reporting.

---

Weakness 4 — Evidence Management

Current Weakness:
Evidence is not consistently organized or mapped to controls.

Expected Improvement:
A controlled evidence index linking requirements, controls, evidence owners, evidence locations, review dates, and status.

---

Weakness 5 — Privacy and Continuity Governance

Current Weakness:
Customer-data governance and continuity validation require additional maturity.

Expected Improvement:
Clear ownership, documented requirements, inventories, testing, and recurring review processes.

---

4. Top 5 Recommended Actions

Action 1 — Complete Critical Access Reviews

Priority: Critical

Owner: IAM / IT Security Manager

Suggested Timeline: 0–30 days

Actions

- Identify all relevant systems and applications.
- Prioritize privileged accounts.
- Review user access.
- Confirm business justification for sensitive access.
- Remove unnecessary privileges.
- Document management approval.
- Establish recurring review frequency.

Business Justification

Reduces the possibility of unauthorized or excessive access to critical systems and customer information.

Success Measure

100% of identified high-risk and privileged access reviewed and appropriately approved.

---

5. Establish the Vendor-Risk Management Program

Priority: Critical

Owner: GRC Manager / Procurement

Suggested Timeline: 0–60 days

Actions

1. Create a complete vendor inventory.
2. Identify vendor access to systems and data.
3. Classify vendors by risk.
4. Create due-diligence requirements.
5. Establish security/privacy questionnaire requirements.
6. Define contractual security requirements.
7. Assess critical vendors first.
8. Establish ongoing monitoring and reassessment.

Business Justification

Reduces security and privacy exposure introduced through external organizations.

Success Measure

100% of critical vendors identified and risk-assessed, with remediation plans for significant findings.

---

6. Clear the High-Risk Security Backlog

Priority: High

Owner: Security Manager

Suggested Timeline: 0–45 days

Actions

- Review all overdue security actions.
- Recalculate priority based on current risk.
- Assign accountable owners.
- Establish realistic deadlines.
- Escalate overdue critical actions.
- Verify completed remediation.
- Close findings only after appropriate evidence is available.

Business Justification

Reduces known security exposure and demonstrates that management is actively addressing identified weaknesses.

Success Measure

Zero overdue critical security actions without formally documented risk acceptance or approved remediation plans.

---

7. Build the Security Evidence Program

Priority: High

Owner: GRC Manager

Suggested Timeline: 0–30 days

Actions

Create a centralized evidence index containing:

- Requirement/control
- Evidence description
- Evidence owner
- Location
- Review date
- Evidence status
- Related finding
- Confidentiality classification
- Expiration/review date where applicable

Business Justification

Strong evidence reduces assessment delays and allows DesertTech to demonstrate that controls are not merely documented but operating.

Success Measure

All priority customer-assessment controls mapped to current, reviewable evidence.

---

8. Strengthen Privacy and Business Continuity Governance

Priority: High

Owner: Privacy Lead / Business Continuity Owner

Suggested Timeline: 30–90 days

Privacy Actions

- Complete customer-data inventory.
- Identify processing activities.
- Identify internal and external data access.
- Review retention requirements.
- Review third-party processors.
- Verify applicable privacy obligations.
- Document relevant privacy controls.

Business Continuity Actions

- Identify critical business services.
- Determine recovery requirements.
- Confirm RTO and RPO.
- Review backup arrangements.
- Validate recovery procedures.
- Conduct an appropriate recovery exercise.
- Record lessons learned.

Business Justification

Protects customer information while improving resilience against service disruption.

Success Measure

Critical services have documented recovery requirements and customer-data processing has appropriate governance and ownership.

13. Overall Recommendation

Recommendation: Proceed with the customer security assessment preparation, but treat the identified critical gaps as management priorities rather than attempting to conceal or minimize them.

DesertTech should focus first on access governance and vendor risk because both areas could create significant security and customer impact.

At the same time, management should strengthen remediation tracking, evidence readiness, privacy governance, and business continuity.

The organization should avoid treating the customer assessment as a one-time documentation exercise. The stronger objective is to establish repeatable GRC processes that continue operating after the assessment.

---

14. Executive Conclusion

DesertTech has a workable foundation but currently has several gaps that could reduce customer confidence and increase business risk.

The recommended strategy is:

Identify → Prioritize → Assign → Remediate → Evidence → Test → Report → Improve

Management should receive regular reporting on the highest-risk areas and require accountable owners to demonstrate measurable progress.

If the recommended actions are implemented according to the proposed priorities and timelines, DesertTech should be in a significantly stronger position to demonstrate security governance, control effectiveness, risk awareness, and assessment readiness.
