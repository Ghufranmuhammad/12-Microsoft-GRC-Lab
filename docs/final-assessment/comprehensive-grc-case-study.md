Comprehensive GRC Case Study

DesertTech Solutions — Customer Security Assessment Readiness

«FICTIONAL / EDUCATIONAL PORTFOLIO CASE STUDY

DesertTech Solutions is a fictional UAE technology company created for educational GRC portfolio purposes. This case study does not represent an actual customer assessment, audit, consulting engagement, or regulatory assessment.»

---

1. Executive Summary

DesertTech Solutions is a fictional UAE technology company with approximately 300 employees. The company operates cloud infrastructure, uses multiple third-party vendors, and processes customer personal data.

The company is preparing for a major customer security assessment. A preliminary review identified several areas of concern:

- Several security actions are overdue.
- Access reviews are incomplete.
- There is no formal vendor-risk management program.
- Customer personal data requires stronger privacy governance.
- Cloud infrastructure creates dependency on appropriate security controls.
- Business continuity arrangements require validation.
- Evidence supporting security and compliance claims is not consistently organized.

The objective of this case study is to perform an end-to-end GRC assessment covering risk identification, risk assessment, control mapping, compliance gaps, evidence requirements, vendor risk, privacy, business continuity, remediation, and management reporting.

---

2. Assessment Objectives

The assessment aims to determine:

1. What are DesertTech's most important information-security and compliance risks?
2. Which controls currently address those risks?
3. Where are significant control or compliance gaps?
4. What evidence should be prepared for the customer assessment?
5. Which third-party risks require management attention?
6. What privacy considerations apply to customer personal data?
7. What business continuity risks could affect critical services?
8. What remediation actions should management prioritize?
9. How should the results be communicated to management?

4. Business Context

DesertTech operates technology services using cloud infrastructure and external service providers.

Its 300 employees include technical, business, administrative, and customer-facing personnel.

The company therefore has several important dependencies:

- Cloud platforms
- Corporate identity systems
- Customer applications
- Internal business systems
- Security tools
- External service providers
- Employees with varying levels of system access

A failure in identity management, cloud security, vendor security, or data protection could affect customers and the company's ability to meet contractual and regulatory expectations.

11. Vendor Risk Assessment

Because DesertTech has no formal vendor-risk program, the first step should be to establish a vendor inventory.

Proposed Vendor Classification

Tier| Example| Risk
Tier 1| Cloud provider with critical production infrastructure| Critical
Tier 2| Vendor processing customer personal data| High
Tier 3| Business SaaS provider with limited sensitive access| Medium
Tier 4| Low-impact supplier without system/data access| Low

Vendor Assessment Process

1. Identify all vendors.
2. Determine what systems and data each vendor can access.
3. Classify vendors according to risk.
4. Perform appropriate due diligence.
5. Review security/privacy documentation.
6. Assess contractual requirements.
7. Identify subcontractors where relevant.
8. Record findings.
9. Obtain risk approval.
10. Monitor vendors throughout the relationship.
11. Reassess higher-risk vendors periodically.

Priority

The cloud provider and any vendor processing customer personal data should receive the highest priority.

12. Privacy Considerations

DesertTech processes customer personal data, creating additional governance responsibilities.

The assessment should determine:

- What personal data is collected?
- Why is it collected?
- Where is it stored?
- Who can access it?
- Which vendors process it?
- How long is it retained?
- How is it protected?
- When is it shared?
- How is it securely deleted?
- What privacy rights and obligations apply?

Key Privacy Risk

A vendor may process customer personal data without sufficient security and privacy due diligence.

Recommended Action

Create a data inventory and map personal-data processing activities to:

- Purpose
- Data category
- System
- Owner
- Access
- Vendor
- Retention
- Protection requirements
- Applicable privacy obligations

The specific legal requirements should be verified against the current applicable UAE law, regulations, and organizational circumstances.

13. Business Continuity Considerations

Because DesertTech relies heavily on cloud infrastructure, availability is an important business risk.

The company should identify:

- Critical business services
- Supporting applications
- Critical infrastructure
- Key vendors
- Recovery dependencies
- Maximum acceptable downtime
- Maximum acceptable data loss
- Recovery responsibilities

RTO and RPO

For each critical service, management should establish:

RTO: How quickly the service needs to be restored.

RPO: How much recent data the organization can afford to lose.

Required Evidence

- Business continuity plan
- Disaster recovery plan
- Recovery procedures
- Backup evidence
- Recovery test results
- Business impact analysis
- Contact/escalation lists
- Lessons learned from exercises

A plan should not be considered effective simply because a document exists. It should be tested periodically.

14. Key Findings

Finding F-001 — Incomplete Access Reviews

Severity: Critical

Condition:
Access reviews are incomplete.

Risk:
Users may retain unnecessary or inappropriate access to systems and customer information.

Recommendation:
Complete a risk-based access review, prioritize privileged and sensitive access, document management approval, and establish a recurring review schedule.

---

Finding F-002 — No Formal Vendor-Risk Program

Severity: Critical

Condition:
DesertTech does not have a formal third-party risk-management lifecycle.

Risk:
Security, privacy, availability, and compliance risks introduced by vendors may remain unidentified or unmanaged.

Recommendation:
Create a vendor inventory, classification model, due-diligence process, contractual security requirements, monitoring process, and periodic reassessment process.

---

Finding F-003 — Overdue Security Actions

Severity: High

Condition:
Several security actions have exceeded their expected completion dates.

Risk:
Known weaknesses may remain unresolved and could contribute to security incidents.

Recommendation:
Reprioritize the backlog according to risk, assign accountable owners, establish target dates, and escalate overdue high-risk actions to management.

---

Finding F-004 — Weak Evidence Organization

Severity: High

Condition:
Security evidence is not consistently organized.

Risk:
DesertTech may be unable to efficiently demonstrate control effectiveness during the customer assessment.

Recommendation:
Create an evidence index connecting requirements and controls to specific evidence owners, locations, review dates, and status.

---

Finding F-005 — Privacy Governance Requires Strengthening

Severity: High

Condition:
Customer personal data is processed, but privacy governance needs further assessment and documentation.

Risk:
Insufficient data governance could increase privacy, security, and compliance exposure.

Recommendation:
Establish a data inventory, processing records where applicable, privacy responsibilities, retention requirements, access controls, vendor mappings, and evidence of appropriate safeguards.

20. Final Assessment Conclusion

The assessment indicates that DesertTech Solutions has a reasonable foundation for addressing information-security requirements but is not yet fully ready for a major customer security assessment.

The most significant readiness issues are:

1. Incomplete access reviews
2. No formal vendor-risk program
3. Overdue security actions
4. Inconsistent evidence organization
5. Privacy-governance gaps
6. Business-continuity validation requirements

The recommended approach is to address the critical risks first, establish accountable ownership, create measurable remediation plans, collect reliable evidence, and provide management with regular risk and compliance reporting.

The ultimate objective is not merely to pass a customer assessment. DesertTech should use the assessment as an opportunity to establish repeatable GRC processes that continuously identify risk, demonstrate control effectiveness, address weaknesses, and provide management with reliable information for decision-making.
