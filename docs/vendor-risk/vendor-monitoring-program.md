# Third-Party Vendor Ongoing Monitoring Program

## 1. Overview
Continuous third-party risk management extends beyond initial onboarding and due diligence. This document outlines the recurring review cadences, continuous monitoring protocols, and out-of-cycle trigger events required to ensure suppliers maintain adequate security and compliance postures throughout the entire vendor lifecycle.

---

## 2. Risk-Tiered Review Frequencies
Vendors are categorized into four distinct risk tiers based on data sensitivity, access level, and business criticality. Each tier dictates a mandatory ongoing review schedule:

* **Critical Vendors**
  * **Criteria:** Direct access to core production infrastructure, sensitive personal data (e.g., Emirates ID, financial records), or pivotal operational dependency.
  * **Review Frequency:** **Annual review + continuous monitoring** (Real-time telemetry, automated posture checks, and annual recertification of security attestations like SOC 2 and ISO 27001).

* **High-Risk Vendors**
  * **Criteria:** Access to internal networks or confidential corporate data, moderate operational dependency, or handling regulated PII.
  * **Review Frequency:** **Annual review** (Comprehensive yearly assessment including questionnaire validation and control sample testing).

* **Medium-Risk Vendors**
  * **Criteria:** Limited network access, non-sensitive business data handling, or easily replaceable services with robust compensating controls.
  * **Review Frequency:** **Every 18 to 24 months** (Periodic reassessment of security posture and compliance documentation).

* **Low-Risk Vendors**
  * **Criteria:** No access to internal systems or sensitive data (e.g., standard public SaaS tools, physical office suppliers).
  * **Review Frequency:** **Risk-based / periodic** (Evaluated upon contract renewal or if business scope changes).

---

## 3. Out-of-Cycle Review Triggers
In addition to scheduled periodic reviews, any of the following high-impact events will immediately trigger an out-of-cycle vendor risk assessment and mandatory security audit:

* **Major Security Incident:** Any confirmed security event or compromise affecting the vendor's operating environment.
* **Data Breach:** Unauthorized access, exfiltration, or loss of corporate or client data stored or processed by the vendor.
* **Change in Ownership:** Mergers, acquisitions, corporate restructuring, or changes in controlling executive management.
* **Major Service Change:** Significant modifications to the vendor's software architecture, cloud hosting provider, or core service delivery model.
* **New Subcontractor:** Onboarding or utilization of unvetted fourth-party sub-processors handling sensitive data workflows.
* **Regulatory Change:** Introduction of new legal or compliance mandates (such as updates to UAE data protection laws) requiring immediate control adjustments.
* **Significant Control Failure:** Discovery of a critical vulnerability, failed audit finding, or lapse in core security certifications (e.g., expired ISO 27001 or unaddressed penetration test findings).
