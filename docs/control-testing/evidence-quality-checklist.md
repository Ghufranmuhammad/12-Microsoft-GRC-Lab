# Evidence Quality Checklist

To ensure compliance testing is reliable and audit-ready, all collected evidence must be evaluated against six core quality pillars:

1. **Relevance:** Does the evidence directly demonstrate the operation of the specific control being tested?
2. **Completeness:** Does the artifact contain all necessary details, parameters, and scopes without missing data?
3. **Authenticity:** Can we establish the trusted source, system origin, or creator of the evidence?
4. **Accuracy:** Is the factual information correct and free of manual tampering or logical errors?
5. **Timeliness:** Was the evidence captured during the exact testing period in scope?
6. **Traceability:** Can we connect the artifact directly to the control ID, evidence owner, and review period?

---

### 5 Examples of Weak Evidence vs. Better Evidence

* **Example 1**
  * ❌ **Weak:** "MFA is enabled."
  * ✅ **Better:** "Microsoft Entra MFA configuration export showing Conditional Access policy enforcement for all 15 privileged administrator accounts, captured on August 22, 2026."

* **Example 2**
  * ❌ **Weak:** "We checked the backups."
  * ✅ **Better:** "Infrastructure quarterly backup restoration log demonstrating successful database recovery in a non-production sandbox environment on July 15, 2026, signed off by the lead system engineer."

* **Example 3**
  * ❌ **Weak:** "Training was done."
  * ✅ **Better:** "LMS annual security awareness training completion report showing 100% completion status across all active personnel, exported on August 1, 2026."

* **Example 4**
  * ❌ **Weak:** "Scans look good."
  * ✅ **Better:** "Microsoft Defender vulnerability scan report dated August 10, 2026, paired with closed Jira patch ticket IDs and patch deployment timestamps."

* **Example 5**
  * ❌ **Weak:** "Policies are updated."
  * ✅ **Better:** "Approved Information Security Policy v3.2 document featuring the GRC Committee meeting minutes and executive sign-off approval dated June 12, 2026."
