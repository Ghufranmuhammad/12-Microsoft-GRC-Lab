## 1. Purpose & Scope
The Control Testing Methodology defines the standardized, repeatable framework utilized across the enterprise to evaluate, test, and validate internal security and compliance controls. This methodology ensures full compliance with international standards (ISO/IEC 27001, NIST SP 800-53) and regional regulatory mandates (such as the UAE Personal Data Protection Law [UAE PDPL] and NCA ECC).

---

## 2. Core Evaluation Framework
Every control evaluated within the enterprise governance framework must be assessed across six foundational dimensions:

### 2.1 Control Objective (What risk is the control supposed to address?)
* **Definition:** The specific security outcome, compliance mandate, or risk mitigation goal that the control is engineered to achieve.
* **Evaluation Criteria:** Auditors must clearly identify the specific threat vector or vulnerability (e.g., unauthorized privilege escalation, data exfiltration, regulatory non-compliance) the control counteracts. An effective control must directly map to an identified enterprise risk in the Risk Register.

### 2.2 Control Design (Is the control appropriately designed to address that risk?)
* **Definition:** An evaluation of whether the control's mechanical architecture and operational mechanics, if executed properly, would successfully prevent, detect, or correct the targeted risk.
* **Evaluation Criteria:** Assessment of policy language, system configurations, automated logic gates, and separation of duties. Design inadequacy results in an immediate design failure regardless of operational performance.

### 2.3 Operating Effectiveness (Is the control actually operating as intended?)
* **Definition:** Verification that the control functions consistently, uniformly, and continuously throughout the designated audit period without unauthorized bypasses.
* **Evaluation Criteria:** Testing whether routine operations, automated systems, and human actors consistently execute the control procedure under real-world conditions across sampled instances.

### 2.4 Evidence (What proves the control operated?)
* **Definition:** The collection of objective, verifiable artifacts, system logs, configuration exports, attestation sign-offs, and audit trails demonstrating that the control executed.
* **Evaluation Criteria:** Evidence must be evaluated against four quality dimensions:
  * **Completeness:** Covers the entire population or required sample subset.
  * **Accuracy:** Free from material errors, omissions, or tampering.
  * **Reliability:** Generated by automated, tamper-evident systems or signed by authorized validators.
  * **Relevance:** Directly substantiates the specific control objective.

### 2.5 Testing (How will you test it?)
* **Definition:** The documented, step-by-step audit procedure executed by the testing team to validate control operation.
* **Evaluation Criteria:** Testing procedures combine four primary audit methods:
  * **Inquiry:** Interviewing control owners and process operators.
  * **Observation:** Watching the control being performed in real-time.
  * **Inspection:** Reviewing physical or digital documentation, logs, and artifacts.
  * **Re-performance:** Independently executing the control mechanics to verify output consistency.

### 2.6 Conclusion (Effectiveness Determination)
Following the completion of testing, evaluation of evidence, and root-cause analysis of any anomalies, the auditor must assign one of four formal testing conclusions:

* **Effective:** The control is properly designed and operating as intended throughout the review period with zero material deficiencies or only negligible, immediately remediated exceptions.
* **Partially Effective:** The control design is sound, but isolated or recurring operational deficiencies were identified, requiring a Corrective Action Plan (CAPA) and retesting.
* **Ineffective:** The control exhibits fundamental design flaws or widespread operational failure, failing to mitigate the targeted risk and requiring immediate escalation and redesign.
* **Not Tested:** The control was scoped out of the current audit cycle due to operational dormancy, system decommissioning, or deferral approved by the CISO.
