# Information Security Governance Methodology

## 1. Governance Purpose
The Information Security Governance Methodology establishes the formal structure, accountability frameworks, and operational processes through which the organization directs, monitors, and evaluates its cybersecurity posture. The core objectives are to:
* Align information security strategy directly with overarching business objectives and risk appetite.
* Protect critical enterprise assets, customer data, and intellectual property from emerging cyber threats.
* Ensure regulatory compliance across all operating jurisdictions.
* Foster a resilient culture where security is recognized as an enterprise-wide responsibility rather than a purely technical silo.

---

## 2. Board vs. Management Responsibilities

### Board of Directors (Governance & Oversight)
* **Strategic Oversight:** Approving the overarching enterprise information security strategy and multi-year risk appetite statements.
* **Resource Allocation:** Reviewing and approving the annual cybersecurity budget and capital allocation for high-priority risk mitigation projects.
* **Performance Monitoring:** Receiving quarterly security posture updates, residual risk reports, and material incident briefings from the CISO.
* **Fiduciary Accountability:** Ensuring management establishes robust compliance frameworks aligned with applicable legal and regulatory mandates.

### Executive Management (Execution & Implementation)
* **Operational Execution:** Translating board-approved risk tolerances into operational security policies, standards, and technical controls.
* **Resource Deployment:** Allocating personnel, tooling, and infrastructure necessary to maintain day-to-day security operations and incident readiness.
* **Risk Integration:** Embedding security requirements into enterprise architecture, procurement workflows, and product development lifecycles.
* **Escalation Management:** Ensuring timely communication of material risks and incidents to the board of directors.

---

## 3. CISO Responsibilities
The Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) acts as the primary strategist and operational leader for the enterprise information security program:
* **Strategy & Architecture:** Designing, implementing, and continually updating the comprehensive enterprise security roadmap.
* **Risk Management:** Directing organization-wide risk assessments, vulnerability management lifecycles, and threat intelligence operations.
* **Incident Leadership:** Serving as the executive incident commander for major security events and orchestrating containment and recovery strategies.
* **Compliance & Audit:** Partnering with internal and external auditors to validate control effectiveness and drive remediation programs.
* **Evangelism & Culture:** Championing security awareness training across all business units and reporting directly to executive leadership and the board.

---

## 4. GRC Analyst Responsibilities
Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) analysts operationalize the governance framework on a daily basis:
* **Policy Lifecycle Management:** Drafting, reviewing, and publishing security policies, standards, and procedural guidelines in coordination with legal and HR.
* **Risk Register Maintenance:** Administering the enterprise GRC platform, logging identified risks, and tracking mitigation milestones.
* **Compliance Assessments:** Conducting periodic control testing, third-party vendor risk evaluations, and regulatory gap analyses.
* **Audit Coordination:** Acting as the liaison between technical engineering teams and external auditors during compliance certifications (e.g., ISO 27001, SOC 2, NIST CSF).

---

## 5. Risk Ownership
Risk ownership follows the "Three Lines of Defense" model, ensuring clear operational accountability:
* **First Line (Business Operations):** Department heads and system owners own the operational risks inherent in their daily processes and applications, and are responsible for implementing prescribed controls.
* **Second Line (Risk & Compliance):** Security and GRC teams own the risk framework, define policy standards, provide risk oversight, and challenge business units on residual risk acceptance.
* **Third Line (Internal Audit):** Independent internal audit provides objective assurance on the effectiveness of governance, risk management, and internal controls.
* **Risk Acceptance Rules:** Acceptance of high or critical residual risks requires formal sign-off from the Executive Risk Committee and the CISO.

---

## 6. Accountability
Accountability is enforced through transparent metrics and role-based performance indicators:
* **RACI Framework:** Every security process, policy, and control is mapped using a RACI matrix (Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed) to eliminate ambiguity during normal operations and crises.
* **Performance Reviews:** Information security adherence and risk management KPIs are integrated into annual performance evaluations and executive scorecards.
* **Consequence Management:** Non-compliance with security policies is subject to standardized disciplinary procedures enforced in partnership with Human Resources and Legal.

---

## 7. Escalation
A standardized escalation matrix ensures rapid communication of emerging threats and control failures:
* **Tier 1 (Operational):** Automated alerts and minor anomalies handled by frontline SOC analysts and system administrators within operational SLAs.
* **Tier 2 (Tactical):** Unresolved incidents, recurring vulnerabilities, or policy violations escalated to Incident Managers and Security Leads within 4 hours.
* **Tier 3 (Strategic / Executive):** Critical incidents, regulatory breaches, or severe third-party exposures escalated immediately (< 15 mins) to the CISO, Executive Management, and the Legal department.

---

## 8. Reporting
Structured reporting cadences maintain transparency across all organizational tiers:
* **Operational Dashboards:** Real-time SIEM, EDR, and vulnerability management metrics tracked continuously by security engineering teams.
* **Monthly GRC Reports:** Summaries of open audit findings, policy exceptions, training compliance rates, and vendor risk scores submitted to department heads.
* **Quarterly Board Briefings:** Executive-level digests covering macro threat landscapes, residual risk trends, regulatory compliance status, and ROI on security investments presented to the Board of Directors.

---

## 9. Decision Tracking
All governance, risk acceptance, and policy exception decisions are managed through formal audit-ready workflows:
* **GRC Ticketing Integration:** Every risk exception request, policy waiver, or remediation task must be logged within the centralized GRC platform with explicit expiration dates.
* **Audit Trail:** Decision records must capture the business justification, risk assessment results, compensating controls, and digital sign-offs from authorized owners.
* **Periodic Review:** Expiring exceptions and open remediation action items undergo mandatory automated re-evaluation 30 days prior to their sunset date.
