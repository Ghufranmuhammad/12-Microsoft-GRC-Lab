# Information Security Governance Committee (ISGC) Charter

## 1. Committee Purpose
The Information Security Governance Committee (ISGC) serves as the primary strategic oversight body for the organization’s information security, privacy, and technology risk management programs. Its purpose is to ensure that security initiatives are aligned with enterprise business objectives, regulatory requirements, and the organization's overarching risk appetite, while driving a culture of security awareness across all departments.

## 2. Scope
The scope of the ISGC encompasses all corporate entities, subsidiaries, business units, third-party service providers, and technology environments processing or storing organizational data. This includes:
* Enterprise cybersecurity strategy and policy approval.
* IT and security risk management oversight.
* Data privacy and regulatory compliance initiatives.
* Major security project funding and resource prioritization.
* Incident response governance and crisis management oversight.

## 3. Membership
The ISGC is a cross-functional executive body. Current membership includes:
* **Chairperson:** Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)
* **Vice-Chair:** Chief Information Officer (CIO)
* **Core Members:**
  * Chief Risk Officer (CRO)
  * Chief Legal Counsel (or designated Senior Privacy Officer)
  * Chief Human Resources Officer (CHRO)
  * Head of Physical Security / Facilities
  * Select Business Unit Managing Directors (rotating annually)
* **Advisors (Non-Voting):**
  * Director of Internal Audit
  * GRC Director / Lead

## 4. Meeting Frequency
* **Standard Cadence:** The ISGC shall meet on a bi-monthly (every two months) basis.
* **Ad-Hoc Meetings:** The Chairperson may convene emergency sessions within 24 hours in the event of a Critical severity security incident, major regulatory breach, or significant shift in the external threat landscape.

## 5. Quorum
A quorum is required for the ISGC to formally approve policies, accept high-level risks, or mandate enterprise-wide corrective actions.
* A quorum is established when **a simple majority (51%) of the voting members** are present, which must strictly include either the CISO or the CIO.
* Proxies may be designated in writing prior to the meeting, provided the proxy holds a director-level title or above.

## 6. Responsibilities
The ISGC is formally responsible for:
* **Strategic Alignment:** Reviewing and approving the annual and multi-year enterprise security roadmap and budget allocation.
* **Policy Governance:** Serving as the final approval authority for all enterprise-level security, privacy, and acceptable use policies.
* **Risk Oversight:** Reviewing the enterprise risk register, challenging risk mitigation timelines, and formally accepting residual risks categorized as High or Critical.
* **Compliance Monitoring:** Tracking the progress of remediation efforts associated with external audits (e.g., SOC 2, ISO 27001), regulatory exams, and penetration tests.
* **Performance Evaluation:** Monitoring core security metrics (e.g., MTTR, phishing click rates, patching SLAs) to assess the operational health of the security program.

## 7. Decision Rights
* The committee operates primarily on consensus.
* In scenarios where consensus cannot be reached, decisions will be carried by a majority vote of present members.
* The CISO holds veto power over decisions that present an immediate, catastrophic cyber risk to the organization, though such vetoes automatically trigger an escalation to the Board of Directors.
* The formal acceptance of any Critical risk requires unanimous consent from the CISO, CRO, and CIO.

## 8. Escalation Rules
* **Operational Blockers:** Security engineering or compliance roadblocks spanning multiple departments are escalated to the ISGC for unblocking.
* **Incident Escalation:** Any incident declared as a "Severity: Critical" under the Incident Response Policy must be escalated to the ISGC members immediately via out-of-band communication, bypassing standard meeting cadences.
* **Board Escalation:** Unresolved disputes within the ISGC regarding risk tolerance, or regulatory failures carrying material financial impact, are escalated directly by the Chair to the Board’s Risk & Audit Committee.

## 9. Reporting
* The ISGC Secretariat (typically managed by the GRC team) is responsible for recording formal minutes, tracking action items, and logging risk acceptance decisions in the enterprise GRC platform.
* The CISO shall prepare a quarterly executive summary derived from ISGC proceedings to be presented to the Board of Directors.

## 10. Review Cycle
This charter, along with ISGC membership rosters and quorum definitions, shall be reviewed and ratified **annually** during the Q1 meeting to ensure continued alignment with organizational restructuring and evolving governance requirements.
